# Tone: learn expression choices, not a personality label

Use with [teach](teach.md) and [write](write.md). A method describes this author's writing in its confirmed scope, not a universal personality. Tone must transfer to new facts without changing what is true, who says it, or how certain it is. Do not impose a house style such as always restrained, always warm, or always short sentences.

## Learn from authored passages

1. **Separate voices before counting.** Identify the author's prose, quoted sources, attachments, boilerplate and extraction artifacts. A signatory is not necessarily the writer; use the user's authorship information. Count independent documents, not pages, repeated phrases or attached records. Do not learn a quotation's emotion, a supplier's sales pitch, or a PDF line break as the author's preference. If a relevant authored passage is unreadable, disclose the gap rather than judging it. Do not inspect unrelated personal attachments just to increase sample coverage.
2. **Compare like functions and preserve their grouping.** Mark the purpose of passages: orient, state facts, explain, acknowledge a difficulty, disagree, recommend, request, conclude, or another function actually present. Trace parent section → subtopic → related moves, noting separately whether the author prints a heading. Compare requests with requests and explanations with explanations, including variations in grouping across documents. A forceful rebuttal and a warm opening can belong to the same method; neither is the default for every paragraph. A move is not automatically a section or a physical heading.
3. **Observe dimensions independently.** Use the questions below as lenses, not mandatory fields or numerical scores. Leave unsupported dimensions unknown. Describe both stable choices and meaningful variation; do not flatten everything into “professional, clear, polite.”

| Dimension | What to observe |
|---|---|
| Speaker and reader relationship | Who speaks for whom; first/third person; distance, solidarity, deference and reader guidance |
| Register | Conversational/formal vocabulary, contractions, technical language and explanation of terms |
| Directness | How soon a point or request appears; explicit action versus indirect suggestion |
| Advocacy and evaluation | Neutral reporting versus positive/negative judgment; intensity of evaluative words and their support |
| Certainty | Fact versus attributed claim, inference, intention, possibility and promise; where hedges or conditions occur |
| Warmth and emotion | Empathy, personal motivation, frustration, enthusiasm or deliberate distance, and where these appear |
| Concession and disagreement | Whether a difficulty is acknowledged; how the writer qualifies, reframes or rebuts it |
| Rhythm and syntax | Short versus extended sentences by function; clause links, parallelism, repetition and paragraph density |
| Wording and transitions | Recurring expression choices, causal/contrastive links, literal phrases versus their communicative function |
| Evidence and request style | How closely support sits to a claim; how the writer guides the reader and asks for an outcome |

Extraction is description before editing. Explicit supported praise, a chain of reasons, reader guidance and a return to an earlier point may be deliberate expression. For recurrence, identify what the later occurrence does (connect evidence, change scale, resolve a concern, support a decision) and where it occurs. Do not remove it just because a shorter version is possible; equally, do not prescribe these traits for an author who does not exhibit them. Distinguish an evidence-backed judgment from a guarantee, and purposeful restatement from accidental duplication.

4. **Make an evidence ledger.** For each candidate choice, record anonymous supporting and opposing sample IDs, applicable independent documents, the passage function and relevant context. Keep passage locations and any original excerpts only in the user's private working area, never in the stored method. Distinguish observed choices, explicit user instructions and proposed corrections. Non-use of a phrase is not evidence that the author forbids it. An unusual passage is evidence of a possible contextual choice, not automatically a global habit. Repeated boilerplate alone is weak evidence of broader voice. Five similar letters do not establish preferences for another language, audience or document type.
5. **Write conditional, executable rules.** State the context/function, the expression action, its boundary and how to recognize compliance. For example, “When answering a documented concern, acknowledge it, then state disagreement with nearby support; do not invent a concern in an ordinary request.” This is a rule shape, not a default style to impose. Explain contradictory examples using an observed condition if possible; otherwise keep the observation mixed and the preference tentative. Do not invent a condition merely to explain away a counterexample. Frequency does not turn a preference into a requirement. Unsupported certainty, factual mistakes and accidental grammar errors are not style permissions; retain an honest observation but propose correction separately, rather than pretending the source was cautious or error-free.
6. **Encode, then check transfer before confirmation.** Build and locally validate the complete method JSON as in [teach](teach.md), then round-trip that exact file through a fresh generator with only the method, writing instructions and new facts. A list of observations or a draft made while looking at the source samples does not test what the schema retained. Check at least a normal case, a context that activates a conditional rule, a context that does not, and missing evidence. The method must not invent material just to activate a signature move. In the private review, trace each consequential ledger choice → observation ID → executable rule/move and its placement → draft passage (or justified non-application). Locate loss at extraction, encoding or execution; do not patch a draft and call the unchanged method repaired.
7. **Collect actual calibration separately.** Show a fitting and an off-tone version of a small invented situation with the *same* actors, facts, time, negation, modality, commitments and available evidence. Change expression, not truth or strength of evidence. Explain the contrast using candidate rules, not “sounds better”; repair factual differences before showing it. Ask whether the fitting wording sounds like the user. Keep the user's choice, any reason they actually give, and the model's assessment separate. Disagreement is a result, not permission to overwrite the user's preference or invent why they chose it. A choice without a reason supports that choice, not a generalized rule about its cause. Without a user response, label calibration pending; generated examples and model votes are not observational evidence or user confirmation.

If there are enough independent examples, reserve one before extracting rules and compare the learned method with it afterward. If all examples have already been used, call the check transfer to a new task, not a holdout. Never report a style accuracy or a successful blind comparison without actually collecting that evidence. A user's correction during calibration is a stated preference, not another observed sample.

## Keep the existing method shape

Do not add new schema fields, a mandatory tone vector or fixed industry categories:

- `expression.voice`: speaker relationship, register, stance, warmth and certainty rules supported by the evidence.
- `expression.language`: syntax, rhythm, transitions and wording choices; preferred/avoided phrases only when justified, with usage limits rather than mandatory phrase stuffing.
- `blueprint.sections[*].purpose`, `moves` and `rules`: preserve the observed parent grouping and subtopic relationships. A move's `instruction` can describe several related actions within a subtopic (evidence → explanation → bounded judgment), not an old conclusion. Use section-local rules for placement and recurrence; use `presentation.format_rules` to say which logical labels are printed as headings or subheadings and which remain implicit. Keep the required nonempty `heading` label even when it is not printed. Neither one section per move nor a fixed small section count preserves hierarchy by itself.
- `when` and `blueprint.conditions`: encode contextual applicability from inputs for the *new* document. An unknown condition is not permission to apply the rule. Do not create a new scope/variant for every local change in intensity.
- `learning.observations`: describe support, counterevidence, scope limits and uncertainty; use existing counts, `assessment` and `basis_ids`. A single occurrence can be tentative; a repeated pattern is still limited to the contexts observed. For `confirmed_instruction`, use the existing user-stated semantics, not fabricated sample counts.
- `expression.examples`: keep a few compact synthetic examples that clarify a difficult choice. Put the fitting text in `text`, mark it `synthetic`, and explain the context and contrast in `notes`; link to the underlying observations. Prefer invented examples over redacting distinctive source passages. Never include case facts, source quotations, real names or identifying combinations. Examples demonstrate expression, not facts to reuse or additional evidence of authorship.

In the private ledger, keep observation, contextual variation, evidence limits, user instruction, proposed safe transformation and actual calibration distinguishable. These are review labels, not new schema fields or observation kinds. Put observed variation/limits in observation `description` and `assessment`; leave unsupported preferences unencoded. Record a user-stated instruction as `confirmed_instruction` / `user_stated` with zero counts and empty `sample_refs`. Keep a proposed correction explicitly proposed in review notes, not as observed or user-confirmed voice. If encoded, identify it as a proposed safe transformation in the relevant constraint/rule `instruction`; its basis may identify the observed issue but does not establish user approval. Fact, legal and safety boundaries apply regardless of preference; never encode permission for an unsafe claim while waiting for calibration.

Before transfer, consolidate overlapping instructions across voice, language, section rules and moves. Give each behavior one executable home, with the union of its relevant `basis_ids`; other placements should describe their distinct function, not demand another performance. Specify what a restatement accomplishes and where it belongs instead of requiring a mini-summary after every fact. Keep separate rules when their triggers or boundaries differ. Each rule must add an observable choice or boundary; do not fill dimensions for completeness. The user's exact-method confirmation still precedes saving.

## Apply and check on a new document

Before drafting, map each planned passage to its function, current evidence and applicable voice rules. Preserve stable voice while selecting context-specific choices; don't apply every learned trait everywhere. Check style compatibility when the requested language, reader, speaker or document type changes; offer adaptation or a new teaching scope instead of claiming an unobserved style was learned.

After drafting, inspect the actual sentences against the rules:

- Is the right person speaking to the right reader, with the learned relationship and level of directness?
- Are warmth, evaluation, rhythm and disagreement placed where the method supports them, rather than replaced by generic polish?
- Are subtopics still grouped under their parent, with connected reasoning and functional returns where learned? Did overlapping rules cause repeated summaries, or did shortening erase an evidenced judgment or useful reader guidance?
- Did a conditional move appear without its trigger, or disappear when its trigger is present?
- Did a stronger adjective, a softened request or a convenient transition change a fact, negation, intention, commitment, uncertainty or logical inference?
- Does each evidence pointer refer to material actually supplied for this document? Are any old facts, legal claims, quotations or synthetic-example details being reused?
- Does the draft preserve the expression behavior without mechanically repeating stock phrases or importing sample mistakes?

Fix factual drift first, then expression drift. If a desired effect cannot be supported by today's materials, keep the gap or uncertainty and explain the limit; never manufacture support. A one-off wording edit does not silently revise the method. Show substantive preference changes through the normal teach-and-confirm flow.
